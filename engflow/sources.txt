Sources behind the calculator defaults

Everything here is an industry benchmark or an illustrative estimate, not EngFlow customer results.

Headcount growth
Company database CSV (local file). Median YoY employee growth by funding stage, 99 rows with EngFlow left out. D+ combines Series D, Series F and Late Stage. Seed has no rows, so it uses Series A.

Build minutes (2.7, range 0.63 to 8.1)
- CircleCI 2025 State of Software Delivery: https://circleci.com/landing-pages/assets/2025-state-of-software-delivery-report.pdf
  Median workflow duration of 2m43s. I couldn't read the PDF text, so I confirmed the median through the next two links instead.
- CircleCI press release: https://circleci.com/newsroom/circleci-publishes-2025-state-of-software-delivery-report
  Seen in search results only.
- The New Stack: https://thenewstack.io/?p=22780982 (also https://thenewstack.io/the-roi-of-speed-how-fast-code-delivery-saves-millions/)
  Gives the quartiles: 25% of teams finish in under 38 seconds, 75% in 8 minutes or less (the report table says 8m7s). Seen in a search extraction only, because the page itself wouldn't load properly.
The median is the number used. The mean looks higher (about 11 minutes, but I couldn't confirm which column that was), and large monorepos are often longer.

CI compute spend ($30 per engineer per month, range $15 to $75)
- cicdcalculator: https://cicdcalculator.com/
  For a 20-developer team it estimates $300 to $1,500 a month. That gives $15 and $75 per developer.
- Semaphore: https://semaphore.io/blog/enterprise-ci-cd-free
  Says a 50-person team with complex pipelines pays about $1,500 a month on GitHub Actions or GitLab and about $2,500 on CircleCI. $1,500 / 50 gives the $30. It's a CI vendor writing "based on our experience", so treat it as an estimate.
- Infratally: https://infratally.com/articles/github-actions-vs-circleci-vs-codepipeline-cost-2026/
  Cross-check only. Working from its assumptions I get about $900 a month for 50 developers, or $18 each.
I dropped the LeanOps article. It claimed $3,000 to $8,000 a month for 50 people without a source, and its own table didn't match.

Savings rates (placeholders; each one is a single company's result)
- Mozilla blog, Firefox/Bazel part 2: https://blog.mozilla.org/nfroyd/2019/11/01/evaluating-bazel-for-building-firefox-part-2/
  Conservative 0.27: Pinterest says "time to land code" fell 27% after its iOS move to Bazel.
  Optimistic 0.83: Dataform's average CI build went from 30 minutes to 5 with Bazel and remote caching.
- Plaid case study, published by EngFlow: https://www.engflow.com/caseStudies/plaid
  Expected 0.50: Plaid cut CI build times by 50% after moving 150+ Go services to Bazel and adding an EngFlow remote cache. The page doesn't say how much came from each change.

AI multipliers (1.0 / 1.24 / 2.0)
These all measure pull request volume, not CI runs. I found nothing that measures CI builds per developer, so the link to runs is an assumption.
- TechRepublic on a Microsoft study: https://www.techrepublic.com/article/news-ai-coding-agents-microsoft-pull-requests/
  Assisted 1.24, from about +24% merged PRs per engineer per day (range +14.5% to +33.7%). The site blocks automated fetching, so I only saw this in search results, and I didn't check the Microsoft study itself.
- Jellyfish: https://jellyfish.co/blog/2025-ai-metrics-in-review/
  PRs per engineer rose 113% (1.36 to 2.9) going from 0% to 100% adoption. They don't define adoption.
- Faros AI: https://www.faros.ai/blog/ai-software-engineering
  High-adoption teams merge 98% more pull requests.
Jellyfish and Faros together are the basis for the heavy-agentic 2.0, which is a placeholder.

No outside source
- Runs per engineer per day (4): placeholder. Replace with EngFlow telemetry. CircleCI reports 1.64 runs per project per day, which can't be converted to per engineer.
- Room-to-improve (1.0) and loading factor (1.0): judgment values.
- Waiting factor (0.30), working days (230), hours per year (1,840): from the EngFlow model spec in context.md.

Looked at but not used
- Bitrise build cache post (83% on a Bazel app): https://bitrise.io/blog/post/bitrise-build-cache-reduces-circleci-build-times. Only seen as a search snippet, and Dataform already covers the 83%.
- devops.com on the 2022 CircleCI report (median about 4 minutes): https://devops.com/?p=139982. This was where the old 4-minute default came from. Replaced by 2.7.
- Salary-by-stage pages (Levels.fyi and similar). Nothing gave a fully loaded cost by stage, and salary is a visitor input anyway.
- A 2026 arXiv study of industrial code repositories: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.12153. Codebase size was dropped from the model.
